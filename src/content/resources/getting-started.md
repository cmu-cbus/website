Computational biology combines computing, math, and biology to understand living systems. As biological data grows in size and complexity, computational methods are essential for answering questions that would otherwise be difficult or impossible to tackle.

If you are interested in exploring computational biology at CMU, here's where to start.

## Take the minis!

**02-180: Great Ideas in Computational Biology I** and **02-181: Great Ideas in Computational Biology II** introduce the algorithms behind modern biological research. They're designed for first-year SCS students, but are also open to other students interested in computational biology. **No prior molecular biology experience is required.**

- **02-180** explores DNA: assembling genomes, comparing sequences, and reconstructing evolutionary relationships.
- **02-181** moves beyond DNA to proteins, neural networks, and algorithms found in nature.

Each is a **5-unit spring mini**. The [CMU course catalog](https://coursecatalog.web.cmu.edu/schools-colleges/schoolofcomputerscience/courses/) lists **02-120 or 15-112**, plus **15-151, 21-127, or 21-128**, as prerequisites for each. Check the [Schedule of Classes](https://enr-apps.as.cmu.edu/open/SOC/SOCServlet) for semester offerings.

## Explore research

Start with the department's [faculty research page](https://www.cmu.edu/cbd/research/index.html) to see examples of ongoing projects, then follow individual faculty homepages to learn more. The [faculty directory](https://www.cmu.edu/cbd/people/voting-faculty.html) is also the starting point recommended by the department's undergraduate research instructions.

**CB majors are guaranteed at least one semester of research for credit** under the department's [research pledge](https://www.cmu.edu/cbd/education/undergraduate/bs-computational-biology/). Research is optional, and research credit can count toward a CB elective.

### Get credit through 02-500

The department's [undergraduate research page](https://www.cmu.edu/cbd/education/courses-offered/02-500-info.html) explains the process:

1. **Find a faculty mentor.** Reach out to a faculty member whose work interests you and agree on a semester project.
2. **Submit your proposal.** Use the [department's enrollment form](https://forms.gle/4f6TovyAq8zk7Mas6) to submit a one- or two-paragraph project description, with your CBD mentor's approval and email address for confirmation.
3. **Share your results.** At the end of the semester, submit an approximately three-page report and present your work to peers. The presentation format—poster or talk—varies by semester.

Questions about research credit? Contact **[Dr. DeBlasio](mailto:deblasio@cs.cmu.edu)**, whom the department lists as the contact for 02-500.
